What mbio draws today, and what each drawing needs¶

Research note written for liulab-mbio#574 on 2026-10-17, on the map that founds liulab-molviz. It is the inventory that viewer's first roadmap starts from: every drawing mbio writes, what each shows, what it is drawn from, what a reader can do with it, what it promises, and every call mbio makes to get it.

It describes features and calls only. How mbio.plot lays a drawing out today is left out on purpose, because the viewer starts from first principles. Where a fact below names a file, that is where the drawing is written, not a design to port.

Every claim was read or measured on 2026-10-17, at commit 10e8785. Each carries its evidence: read (the code or a page, at the path given), measured (a script over docs/examples/, byte counts exact), or inferred.

2. What each view of a map shows¶

A map page holds up to three views of one record; a figure and the verification page use the same three. Read from the module docstrings of plot/circular.py, plot/linear.py and plot/sequence_view.py, and plot/layers.py.

View Shows Shown when
Circle The record's name and length, a bp scale, each feature as an arrow along its strand (a box where it has none), each primer at each binding site, each cut site labelled with its enzymes and position, each insertion as a mark labelled with how many bases it adds, and the hidden-label notice A circular record drawn whole
Line The same items along a stretch, numbered as the record numbers it, with • • • at an end the molecule carries on past. A feature the stretch cuts is drawn cut, with no arrowhead at the cut Always on a page; the only shape for a region or a linear record
Sequence view A position ruler, the top strand, a tick rail, the bottom strand, each row's last position. Each primer along its binding site, its 5' tail off the template and each base it does not pair with marked red. Each cut site through each strand where its enzyme cuts that strand, so the overhang shows. Each feature as a bar, with a thin line across the gap a joined feature leaves; a CDS's amino acids in three letters over their codons, a stop in red. Each insertion through both strands. Each track's curves under the rows Up to 100,000 bases; a page leaves it and its switch out past that

4. What a reader can do¶

On a map page (read from plot/plot.js, 668 lines, and plot/page.py):

  1. Hover any item to see its name, type, span and length, 1-based. Hover the notice to list the hidden labels that show.
  2. Switch each kind on or off in place (Features, Primers, Cut sites, Insertions), and each feature type. The notice recounts what hid among what shows.
  3. Flip between circle and line, where both exist, and show or hide the sequence view. Each shape keeps its own zoom and scroll position.
  4. Zoom the map from 1× to 8× by a slider and a reset button, or by Ctrl or ⌘ with the wheel or a trackpad pinch, about the pointer. The circle zooms freely and the line in four steps; a label hidden at 1× can show on a longer line (tests/plot/test_linear.py:342).
  5. Drag to pan the map. A touch scrolls.
  6. Click an item to select it in both views; the other view scrolls to it. Click again to clear.
  7. See the sequence view beside the map when the window has room, and under it otherwise, in rows half as long where full ones would not fit.
  8. Toggle the bottom strand.
  9. Hover a base to see its position. Drag across bases to select them, the view scrolling while the pointer is past its edge; the caption says first .. last (N bp). Copy puts the top strand's selected bases on the clipboard, by the button or the copy keys.
  10. Print: the switches and zoom caption drop, the full rows and the line at 1× print.

There is no find box, no reverse-complement copy, no keyboard control and no export from the page (read: plot.js has no keydown handler). The browser's own find reaches labels and the bases within one row, since both are text (inferred from the page's markup).

On a protocol page (read from protocol/render.py:2105-2186, protocol/protocol.js:189-198 and protocol/protocol.css:247-288): a figure is a still drawing. "Explore the map" loads the record's map page into the protocol page the first time it opens, and "Close the map" puts the figure back; "Open it in a new tab" opens the same file. A figure prints small whether its map was open or not. Plates and gels have no control but a gel band's tooltip and a transfer's closed list of moves.

On the verification page: nothing beyond reading and the browser's own find. It has no script (read: verification/page.py:136-182).

5. The promises a reader relies on¶

The map's decisions (issue liulab-mbio#572) keep the first four as needs. Each is pinned by a test where one is named.

  1. No label overlaps another label or the drawing — circle, line and sequence view (tests/plot/test_circular.py:316,321, test_linear.py:222, test_sequence_view.py:363).
  2. A hidden label is counted, by kind, on the drawing, recounted as switches change, listed on hover, and printed by the command after the files it wrote (test_cli.py:69). The sequence view hides nothing. A name on a feature never hides; labels hide cut sites first, the most frequent cutter first, then primers, features and insertions, and unlit before lit (test_drawing.py:1300,1455).
  3. Bases and labels are real text on a page, so they can be found, selected and copied. A PNG and a PDF draw every letter as an outline instead, so they look the same anywhere and need no font (test_drawing.py:274).
  4. One file opens offline: a page loads nothing over the network and embeds the fonts its text was measured in (test_drawing.py:387,397). A protocol page is one file too, but its "Explore the map" opens a sibling *-map.html, so a protocol is a folder.
  5. Positions read as the record numbers them: 1-based and inclusive in every label and tooltip, a region keeps the record's numbering, across the origin too (test_drawing.py:884,1156).
  6. A highlight moves nothing and dims the same way in every view and format (test_drawing.py:1393,1446).
  7. A page carries everything; a file draws only what is on. A page keeps every item behind its switches; a PNG or PDF leaves out what is switched off (test_drawing.py:256,649).
  8. The same input writes the same bytes. scripts/check_examples.py regenerates every example and compares bytes, in the docs CI job. The viewer's output, or that check, has to allow for this.
  9. A drawing keeps a white ground whatever the colour scheme. A map page and the verification page declare a light scheme; a protocol page has a dark one and paints each drawing's ground white (protocol/protocol.css:247-250).

Open gaps¶

  1. The page behaviours in section 4 were read from plot.js, not run in a browser. The gate parses each page's HTML but runs no script (read: no browser or JavaScript runner in tests/ or pyproject.toml), so nothing pins them. The viewer's roadmap needs its own tests of each.
  2. An explore page's cut sites follow its figures' enzymes. A figure naming an empty list writes a map page with no cut sites and no switch for them: the Gibson and Gateway product pages have none (measured: 0 cut-site items). Whether the viewer keeps that rule, or lets the reader add enzymes, is the roadmap's call.
  3. A plate past 10 kinds tells the reader nothing about its wells. The legend drops, every used well takes one colour, and a well's contents ride only as data attributes nothing shows (read: plot/plate.py, and no hover on protocol pages). AP-1's picked plate carries 288 well labels.
  4. Drawings the figure-sources note wants and nothing draws: lines joining one stacked row's cut to the next row's end (iGGA Figure S1A); a plate coloured by a per-well verdict (DMX panel a, step 10); a Sankey of a counted run and a cost chart (DMX panels c and d); a workflow track (docs/research/figure-sources.md). That note says nothing lays out more than one record; stacked figure rows have since shipped, so only the joining lines are missing.
  5. The verification page fakes two kinds. Results ride as primers and disagreements as misc_difference features. The viewer needs a read and a difference as kinds of their own; until then the page inherits primer labels and feature colours for them.
  6. A reader cannot find a sequence across rows, copy the bottom strand, or move by keyboard on any page.
  7. Not measured: PNG output (no example writes one), the time a page takes to open, and how a page behaves on a phone.
  8. The index. docs/research/index.md does not list this note: the branch carries the note alone and is never merged.
