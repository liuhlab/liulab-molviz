What liulab-protein will need a viewer to draw¶

Research note written for liulab-mbio#576 on 2026-10-17, on map liulab-mbio#572. It lists the views liulab-protein, the viewer's second user, would ask liulab-molviz for. Needs only: no design, no package choice.

What was read, on 2026-10-17: liulab-protein's default branch at commit eab571e (committed 2026-09-04): its code, README.md, CONTEXT.md, its eight ADRs, its guides and its research notes; and its whole tracker, 79 issues (all closed) and 16 pull requests (one open, a dependabot bump), with their 180 comments. Read-only: nothing was run against it and nothing was written to it.

Every claim below is read from a named file or thread, or marked inferred. "Today" means 2026-10-17.

1. The answer first¶

# View Starts from What a reader does with it Drawn in liulab-protein today?
1 A structure in 3D, whole entry or one chain Structure.atoms or Chain.atoms, a biotite AtomArray turns and zooms the fold; looks at one chain alone Yes: Structure.view(), Chain.view() over py3Dmol
2 A prediction coloured by its own confidence a Structure from ESMFold2.fold or Structure.from_file; per-residue confidence in the B-factor column sees which parts of the fold the model trusts Yes, by a 3Dmol.js style passed to view()
3 The pairwise confidence matrix as a heatmap, chain boundaries marked Structure.confidence.pairwise(), an (L, L) float32 array judges whether an interface in a complex is real No. Named as the one uncovered gap
4 Per-residue confidence as a line along the chain the B-factor column the same as view 2, flat No, and declined: view 2 covers it
5 An alignment: its rows against the query's columns, and its depth by position an MSA sees whether the alignment is deep where it must be No. Deferred, not declined
6 Sequence search hits the DataFrame from Protein.search ranks hits; sees where on the query each one lands No. A table only
7 Structure search hits the DataFrame from Structure.search or Chain.search the same, plus how well the shapes agree No. A table only
8 Which stretches of a protein have a solved structure the DataFrame from Protein.structures picks the entry and chain to open No. A table only
9 Sparse features along a sequence a SaeActivation, joined to the feature descriptions sees which named features fire where No. A lookup only

Views 1 and 2 exist, so for them the need is replacement, if liulab-protein ever moves off py3Dmol. View 3 is the only one its own tracker names as missing. Views 4 to 9 are what its types hold and no thread asks to see, except view 5, deferred, and view 4, declined.

No protein sequence with domains. A Protein carries no feature or domain annotation at all: its sequence, an accession, an entry name, a description and the FASTA header fields organism, taxon_id, gene, protein_existence and sequence_version (read, src/protein/core.py and src/protein/db/swissprot.py, 2026-10-17). Nothing in the package or its tracker fetches UniProt features or InterPro domains. A sequence view drawn for liulab-protein today would carry the per-residue spans its own types hold instead: the SIFTS ranges (view 8), search-hit ranges (views 6 and 7), per-residue confidence (view 2) and SAE features (view 9).

2. Each view¶

2.1 A structure in 3D¶

Starts from. Structure is one set of coordinates, a PDB entry's asymmetric unit or any coordinate file; Chain is one polymer in it, reached as structure["A"]. .atoms is every atom of the first model, ligands, water and nucleic acids included; .models holds every model of an NMR entry (38 for 1L2Y, per its docstring); .chain_ids lists the labels in file order; Chain.kind is "protein", "nucleic" or "other" (read, CONTEXT.md, src/protein/structure/structure.py, src/protein/structure/chain.py, 2026-10-17).

What is drawn today. protein.structure.view.view(atoms, name=, width=, height=, style=) serialises the atoms as mmCIF text and returns py3Dmol's own view. The default style is one cartoon per chain coloured N to C ({"cartoon": {"color": "spectrum"}}); style={} hands the drawing to the caller. A chain's view writes that chain's atoms, so it shows that chain and not the entry around it (read, src/protein/structure/view.py; merged in PR liulab-protein#96 on 2026-09-04).

What the guides ask the viewer for (read, docs/guides/structures.md, 2026-10-17):

  • turn by dragging, zoom by scrolling;
  • a cartoon coloured N to C, by chain;
  • one chain alone, protein or nucleic (1BNA chain A, one DNA strand);
  • sticks with a carbon colour scheme, and a van der Waals surface at 0.6 opacity;
  • colour by a per-residue number read from the file (view 2).

Where it is shown. Three places (read, ADR-0008, docs/guides/structures.md, docs/start.md, 2026-10-17):

  • a notebook, by .show();
  • a static docs page, by .write_html() returning HTML that markdown-exec writes into the page while the site builds, with no network and no LIULAB_DATA;
  • a page of its own, by .write_html(open_file).

Constraints a replacement inherits (read, ADR-0008 and the module docstrings, 2026-10-17):

  • A view is asked for. Neither class has _repr_html_, because displaying a Structure must not parse its file or reach RCSB.
  • Labels are case-sensitive and may be longer than one character. 10EG holds both A and a; mmCIF is the format because it carries such labels.
  • The file carries no secondary structure. Only atom_site is written, and 3Dmol.js computes the helices and sheets itself.
  • The page holds the coordinates inline, so a large entry makes a large page.
  • Today's page needs a network. py3Dmol loads 3Dmol.js from a CDN. That falls short of the "one file opens offline" promise map liulab-mbio#572 lists for mbio (decided 2026-10-17). Inferred: liulab-protein would gain offline pages from molviz, though it has not asked for them.

2.2 A prediction coloured by its confidence¶

Starts from. A Structure returned by ESMFold2.fold, or one reopened with Structure.from_file. Per-residue confidence is the mmCIF's B-factor column, "where every viewer looks" (read, CONTEXT.md, entry Prediction, 2026-10-17). The scalars are on Structure.confidence, a Confidence(plddt, ptm, iptm, pairwise_file), each scalar 0 to 1 (read, src/protein/fold/predictions.py, 2026-10-17).

Two scales. The B-factor column runs 0 to 100, while Confidence.plddt runs 0 to 1. The docs once coloured with min: 0, max: 1 and painted every residue one colour; PR liulab-protein#97 fixed it (read, PR liulab-protein#97, merged 2026-09-04). Both guides now pass {"prop": "b", "gradient": "roygb", "min": 50, "max": 100}, blue confident and red not (read, docs/start.md, docs/guides/structures.md, 2026-10-17).

A reopened prediction carries no Confidence. On a cache hit, or after from_file, confidence is None while the B-factor column still holds pLDDT. On a crystal structure the same column is a temperature factor, and no value range tells the two apart (read, issue liulab-protein#93 comment of 2026-09-04; docs/guides/folding.md, 2026-10-17). Inferred: a viewer colouring by confidence cannot work out on its own whether the column means confidence; the caller has to say so.

What a prediction holds. Protein, DNA and RNA chains fold together. The getting-started page shows AP-1: FOS and JUN bZIP domains with a DNA duplex, 4 chains and 164 residues (read, PR liulab-protein#97, 2026-09-04). Chain labels come from position in the request, so a caller reads them off chain_ids (read, docs/start.md, 2026-10-17).

2.3 The pairwise matrix¶

Starts from. Structure.confidence.pairwise() reads <name>.pairwise.npy beside the mmCIF and returns (L, L), one entry per pair of residues; lower is surer. It raises FileNotFoundError where the model reported no matrix. The file is written only for a prediction that reported one, and the folding guide calls it the thing that "says whether two chains sit correctly against each other" (read, src/protein/fold/predictions.py, docs/guides/folding.md, 2026-10-17).

Asked for. Issue liulab-protein#93 proposed Structure.plot_pairwise(ax=None), "the (L, L) matrix as a heatmap", with chain boundaries marked (opened 2026-09-04). Its closing comment (2026-09-04) dropped the confidence curve and kept this one: "The pairwise matrix has no substitute … there is no way to look at it … if plotting comes back, it comes back for the heatmap." It closed without filing a follow-up, pending somebody hitting the gap in practice.

What a reader needs on it (read, issue liulab-protein#93 body, 2026-09-04): the matrix, and where one chain ends and the next begins. Inferred: the boundaries come from the prediction's chains in chain_ids order and each chain's residue count; nothing in the code states that the matrix's row order is that order.

2.4 Per-residue confidence as a line¶

Proposed as Structure.plot_confidence(ax=None) in issue liulab-protein#93 (2026-09-04), then dropped in its closing comment the same day: the 3D view coloured by the B-factor column "reads better than a curve". Listed here so the roadmap does not count it as an open need.

2.5 An alignment¶

Starts from. An MSA: (header, row) pairs of A3M text, row 0 the query. .query, .query_header, .depth (rows), .match_states (columns), .to_a3m() and .compress(). Case is the match state: an uppercase residue or - holds a column, and a lowercase residue is an insertion that holds none. Headers are carried byte for byte, because a key=<taxon> field pairs the chains of a complex, and so is a leading # line (read, CONTEXT.md entry MSA, src/protein/msa/msa.py, docs/guides/alignments.md, 2026-10-17). Made by Protein.msa(db) or align(sequences, query=...).

Asked for. Issue liulab-protein#93, under "Not in this issue" (2026-09-04): "An MSA coverage plot, rows against position, is the standard way to see whether an alignment is deep where it needs to be." It was deferred until the alignments guide existed. The guide shipped and points at no picture (read, docs/guides/alignments.md, 2026-10-17).

What a reader needs on it. Depth at each query column, and inferred the rows themselves laid against the query's columns, insertions hidden or marked. The guide's own depth rule, a few thousand rows as a floor, says what a reader checks (read, docs/guides/alignments.md, 2026-10-17).

2.6 Sequence search hits¶

Starts from. Protein.search(db) runs MMseqs2 and returns a pandas.DataFrame, one row per hit: query, target, pident (a percentage), alnlen, mismatch, gapopen, qstart, qend, tstart, tend, evalue, bits. No aligned strings: the package asks the tool for these columns and no others (read, src/protein/external.py, docs/guides/search.md, 2026-10-17).

Inferred need: hits as spans along the query, from qstart and qend, ranked by evalue or bits. Nothing in the code, docs or tracker asks for a picture of a hit table.

2.7 Structure search hits¶

Starts from. Structure.search(db) or Chain.search(db) runs Foldseek. The table has the same columns, with fident (a fraction, not a percentage) for identity, plus alntmscore and lddt, which score how well the shapes agree. query is <entry>_<chain> (read, src/protein/external.py, docs/guides/search.md, 2026-10-17).

A target is not a Structure key. In Foldseek's pdb100 a target is a biological assembly chain such as 1ubq-assembly1_A, and a label may be longer than one character (6dwu-assembly44_DU), while Structure("1UBQ") holds the deposited asymmetric unit (read, docs/research/foldseek-pdb100-contents.md and CONTEXT.md entry Structure, 2026-10-17). pdb100 holds C-alpha traces only and renumbers residues, so coordinates for a hit come from RCSB (read, CONTEXT.md, 2026-10-17). Inferred: a "query and hit side by side in 3D" view needs the target named as an entry and a chain the asymmetric unit carries, which the hit table does not give directly.

No thread asks for a picture of these hits.

2.8 Which stretches of a protein have a structure¶

Starts from. Protein.structures, a DataFrame with one row per SIFTS segment: pdb, chain, accession, res_beg, res_end (the range in the chain) and sp_beg, sp_end (the range in the UniProt sequence). One chain and accession may carry several rows, and the two ranges are not always the same length, so both come back verbatim and no offset is computed (read, CONTEXT.md entry SIFTS, src/protein/sifts.py, docs/concepts.md, 2026-10-17). Chain.uniprot is the other direction, a tuple of none, one or several accessions.

What a reader does. The concepts page walks from an accession to a row to Structure(row["pdb"])[row["chain"]] (read, docs/concepts.md, 2026-10-17). Inferred need: the segments drawn along the UniProt sequence, so a reader picks the entry that covers the residues they care about. Nothing asks for it.

A trap for any view joining sequence to structure. Chain.sequence is the observed sequence: a residue with no coordinates is absent, so a disordered loop is missing rather than filled from SEQRES (read, src/protein/structure/chain.py, 2026-10-17). A chain's residue index, its author numbering and the UniProt position are three different numbers.

2.9 Sparse features along a sequence¶

Starts from. A SaeActivation: indices and values, both (L, k), naming which codebook features fired at each residue and how hard; reconstruction_loss, (L,); .max() for the per-sequence vector; and the facts naming the SAE, its parent, layer and codebook. features.descriptions("6b-layer60-k64-cb16384") returns a label and a description per feature index, for that one SAE only (read, src/protein/embed/esm/sae.py, src/protein/embed/esm/features.py, docs/guides/embedding.md, 2026-10-17).

What a reader does today. Joins by index: frame.loc[activation.indices[0]], "what fired at the first residue" (read, docs/guides/embedding.md, 2026-10-17). Inferred need: a track along the sequence showing where a chosen feature fires, labelled from the descriptions. This is the nearest thing liulab-protein holds to a domain track. Nothing asks for it.

Embeddings themselves (Embedding, (L, d_model)) have no view asked for anywhere, and none is implied beyond what the SAE gives.

3. Where liulab-protein's own stance on drawing came from¶

Read from the tracker, 2026-10-17, in order:

  1. 2026-09-04, issue liulab-protein#92. The docs-site plan noted "Nothing here plots anything" and proposed matplotlib in the docs environment only, pointing readers at ChimeraX or PyMOL for the structure: "A 3D cartoon is not something this package should grow."
  2. 2026-09-04, PR liulab-protein#96. That was overtaken the same day: view() over py3Dmol landed, with ADR-0008. py3Dmol is held rather than wrapped, so a caller gets every 3Dmol.js call. It is the one runtime dependency taken from PyPI rather than conda-forge, because the conda-forge recipe makes IPython a hard dependency.
  3. 2026-09-04, PR liulab-protein#97. The getting-started page ends the fold with the AP-1 prediction in 3D, coloured by confidence.
  4. 2026-09-04, issue liulab-protein#93. The flat plots were closed as not worth their cost, except the pairwise heatmap, left uncovered.

Nothing after that touches drawing. The PRs merged after liulab-protein#97 are a menu change (liulab-protein#98), a fold-request change (liulab-protein#99) and its docs (liulab-protein#100), all on 2026-09-04, the date of the newest commit on the default branch. The one open PR is a dependency bump (read, 2026-10-17).

Open gaps¶

  • Whether liulab-protein moves to molviz at all. ADR-0008 chose py3Dmol on purpose, for the whole 3Dmol.js API at the cost of one small module. Replacing it is that repo's decision. Nothing here asks it.
  • Aligned strings for a hit. The hit tables ask for no aligned-sequence columns. Whether MMseqs2's and Foldseek's --format-output can add them, and whether search's extra pass-through can change the format, was not checked. A hit drawn as an alignment depends on both.
  • Superposing two structures. No code superposes a prediction on its crystal, or a query on a Foldseek hit. The getting-started page's "Where next" says to "open the prediction and the crystal, and look at them in 3D", but the structures guide shows them one at a time. Whether a superposed view is a need is open, and whether Foldseek reports the transform was not checked.
  • Assembly against asymmetric unit. A Foldseek target names an assembly chain, and a Structure holds the asymmetric unit (section 2.7). How a hit is opened in 3D is unresolved in liulab-protein.
  • The pairwise matrix's order. That its rows follow chain_ids and each chain's residues is inferred, not read (section 2.3).
  • Domain annotations. No source exists in liulab-protein. A domain track would need data that package does not fetch today.
  • The published site was not opened. The two viewers on it were read from the page sources and the PRs, not looked at.
