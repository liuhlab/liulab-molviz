Choose what a map shows¶

A map draws its record's features and primers, the cut sites of each enzyme that cuts it once, and its insertions, reads and disagreements. A track's curves show only in the sequence view. This guide leaves out what your reader doesn't need, picks the enzymes, draws a stretch of the record, and lights the names that matter. Each option sets what the drawing starts with. On a page, the reader can still change it with the switches above the map.

The examples draw pUC19-GFP, the pUC19 vector with GFP put in by Golden Gate assembly. Save its record, puc19-gfp-product.json, beside your script, and load it once:

import json
from pathlib import Path

import molviz

record = json.loads(Path("puc19-gfp-product.json").read_text())

Each step shows its code, then the drawing it makes, drawn here from the same record and options. The options table lists every option with its default.

Leave out a kind¶

To leave out everything of one kind, name the kind in kinds_off. The kinds are features, primers, cuts, insertions, reads, disagreements and tracks. This map leaves out the primers:

molviz.draw(record, kinds_off=["primers"]).write("no-primers.html")

Leave out feature types¶

To leave out features by their type, name the types in types_off. This map leaves out the M13 sites, which are primer_bind features, and the MCS and the two junctions, which are misc_feature:

molviz.draw(record, types_off=["primer_bind", "misc_feature"]).write("fewer-features.html")

types_off starts as ["source"], and your list replaces it. If the record has a source feature that you want left out, name source in your list too.

Name the enzymes to draw¶

A map draws the cut sites of each enzyme that cuts the record once. To draw other enzymes, name them in enzymes. BsaI cuts pUC19-GFP twice, so its sites are left out until you name it:

molviz.draw(record, enzymes=["BsaI"]).write("bsai.html")

Your list replaces the default, so this map draws BsaI's sites and no others. A name matches in any case. molviz draws only the cuts that your record lists, so an enzyme with no cut in the record draws nothing.

Draw the record as a line¶

A circular record drawn whole opens on the circle. To draw it as a line, set map to "line":

molviz.draw(record, map="line").write("line.html")

Draw a stretch of the record¶

To draw only some of the bases, give a span with a start and an end. start counts from 0, and the base at end is not drawn. A span is drawn as a line, numbered as the record is. This span holds GFP and the bases on either side of it:

molviz.draw(record, span={"start": 300, "end": 1250}).write("gfp-span.html")

On a circular record, end may pass the record's length to run across the origin. pUC19-GFP has 3,347 bases, so this span holds its last 247 bases and then its first 153:

molviz.draw(record, span={"start": 3100, "end": 3500}).write("origin-span.html")

A span runs at most once around the record. The circle draws a record whole, so map="circle" with a shorter span is an error. Coordinates in the record format gives the rules for counting bases.

Light the names that matter¶

To light some items and dim the rest, name them in highlight. All else turns one pale grey, and nothing moves. This map lights GFP and the two primers that copy it:

molviz.draw(record, highlight=["GFP", "GFP forward", "GFP reverse"]).write("gfp-lit.html")

A name can be a feature, a primer, an insertion, a read, a disagreement, a track or an enzyme. An enzyme lights every site it cuts. A name matches in any case. If a name matches nothing in the record, draw raises ValueError with the message options.highlight[0]: names nothing in the record.

Find the hidden labels¶

Where labels would overlap, a drawing hides some of them and counts them in a notice at the bottom right of the map. Labels hide when you ask for more than fits. pUC19-GFP hides nothing, even with every enzyme, so this step draws AP-1's product, ap1-product.json, which holds cuts for 14 enzymes. Drawn on the circle with all of them, some of its cut-site labels hide:

ap1 = json.loads(Path("ap1-product.json").read_text())
every_enzyme = sorted({cut["enzyme"] for cut in ap1["cuts"]})
drawing = molviz.draw(ap1, enzymes=every_enzyme)
print(drawing.notice)
for label in drawing.hidden:
    print(label)

It prints:

6 cut sites are hidden
{'kind': 'cuts', 'name': 'BtgZI', 'at': 1864}
{'kind': 'cuts', 'name': 'BtgZI', 'at': 2233}
{'kind': 'cuts', 'name': 'BtgZI', 'at': 2396}
{'kind': 'cuts', 'name': 'BtgZI', 'at': 2577}
{'kind': 'cuts', 'name': 'BspQI - SapI', 'at': 2295}
{'kind': 'cuts', 'name': 'BspQI - SapI', 'at': 2505}

The drawing's notice is the sentence that the drawing shows, and hidden lists each hidden label in the shape that Hidden labels gives.

On a page, rest the pointer on the notice to list the hidden labels. The page lays the labels out again each time the reader turns the circle or flips a switch, so the count can change:

To bring a hidden label back, draw less: leave out a kind, name fewer enzymes, or draw a span. A lit label is the last to hide, so highlight keeps the labels that matter in view. How a drawing stays readable gives the order in which labels hide.
